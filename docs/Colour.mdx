# Colour & tone

Two layers, and only one of them is an API.

## Primitives are not for you

`color.blue.500` says what a colour *is*. Fifty of them exist, they are defined
in `primitive.tokens.json`, and product code may not touch them. They exist to
define the layer below and for nothing else.

If no semantic role fits what you are building, that is a signal the system is
missing a role — not a licence to reach one layer down. Say so in `DS-GAPS.md`
and stop.

## Semantics are roles

| group | what it answers |
| --- | --- |
| `surface.*` | what is this sitting on |
| `text.*` | how important is this text |
| `border.*` | is this decoration, a control boundary, or focus |
| `action.*` | what does pressing this do |
| `status.*` | what state is this in |

`text.muted` survives a rebrand. `text.grey500` does not.

## Tone is one vocabulary

Five values, in every component that has a tone axis:

`neutral` · `info` · `success` · `warning` · `critical`

A component narrows the scale, never redefines it. `Button` takes `neutral` and
`critical`; `Badge` takes all five; `Progress` takes `neutral`, `success`,
`critical`. Each is declared as an `Extract<Tone, …>` in `vocabulary.ts`, so a
narrowing is provably a subset and adding a sixth tone is one edit.

Until 4.0 this was three separate unions that happened to overlap, and two
components — `Button` and `Progress` — started their scale at `default` instead
of `neutral` and disagreed with each other about which values existed. Nothing
was broken in any one file. It was broken across files, which is the only place
a design system can be broken.

`tone="default"` resolves for the whole 4.x line and is removed in 5.0.

## Selection is not a status

`selection.bg`, `selection.fg` and `selection.mark` — the row the reader
picked, the destination they are on, the tile that is checked.

Six components used to say that with `status.info`, and it was the wrong role
by one word. A status is a message the system is making: *this failed*, *this
saved*, *here is something you should know*. Selection is not news the system
has; it is the answer to a question the reader just asked by clicking. The two
happened to want a tint and a border, so one stood in for the other, and the
tell was that a selected row and an information banner were the same colour —
a page could not distinguish "you are here" from "we have something to tell
you".

It cost a change of accent to notice. When the primary action moved from blue
to green, six components kept a blue current state, and the reason was not that
blue was hard-coded anywhere: it was that they were pointing at a role that had
no reason to follow the accent.

`mark` is the leading bar, and it is the part held to 3:1 on its own. The tint
is the pleasant half of the indication and the first to disappear — in
greyscale, in forced colours, for anyone who cannot separate two close greens.
The bar is what survives, which is why it is a token rather than
`currentColor`.

## Contrast is a build error

Twenty-nine foreground/background pairs are declared in the semantic token
file's `$extensions` block. `src/test/contrast.spec.ts` iterates them across both themes
and fails the build on any that misses its threshold.

The thresholds are not all 4.5:1, and getting that distinction wrong in either
direction is a real failure — too lax ships unreadable UI, too strict forces a
washed-out palette nobody wants:

| requirement | ratio | what it applies to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `bodyText` | 4.5:1 | body copy, button labels, status messages |
| `largeText` | 3:1 | headings above 18.66px bold or 24px regular |
| `nonText` | 3:1 | control boundaries and the focus ring (WCAG 1.4.11) |

Adding a token that renders text or a control boundary means adding a line to
the pairs list. That list is not documentation. An undeclared pair is a review
comment; a declared one is a gate.

## Never colour alone

WCAG 1.4.1. A status whose meaning lives in its hue is invisible to anyone who
cannot see the difference. `Badge` requires its label and has no `color` prop;
`status.*` tokens always accompany text. This is a rule about the component
API, not about how carefully someone uses it.

## Dark theme changes colour and nothing else

`theme.dark.tokens.json` declares colour roles only. Every non-colour semantic
— spacing, sizes, radii, durations — is shared, because a theme that changes
spacing is not a theme, it is a second design.

The build enforces it: a dark override naming a role the semantic layer does
not define throws, and there is no mechanism for a dark-only spacing value.
